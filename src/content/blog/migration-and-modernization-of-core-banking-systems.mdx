Banking is adopting cloud at a never-before-seen pace. There are mounting pressures from customers, market dynamics, and regulatory bodies, and banks find themselves at a pivotal juncture, compelled to embrace modernization strategies to stay relevant and competitive.

### Pressures driving Modernization

Customers are now seeking more than just transactions; they seek** personalized experiences **and holistic treatment. Meanwhile, FinTechs challenge traditional economic models with **lower cost-to-serve customers** and innovative approaches. Regulatory mandates evolve, emphasizing **data control, transparency, and compliance**, leaving banks with no choice but to adapt or risk falling behind.

### Different Paths to Migration and Modernization:

Banks chart diverse courses in their migration and modernization endeavors, with each path offering unique opportunities and challenges. 3 main use cases emerge:

**Augmenting existing core systems** involves building modern digital frontends while retaining legacy infrastructure.

**Migration of core to the cloud**, gradually refactoring to leverage scalability and cost efficiencies. Develop, test, construct, and execute crucial workloads within the cloud environment, expediting transformation through the deployment of applications and digital services. Commence with a lift-and-shift approach initially, followed by subsequent modernization endeavors.

**Replacing core** altogether, launching cloud-native banks independent of legacy structures. Additionally, launching a new bank emerges as a viable option, enabling fresh approaches untethered from legacy constraints.

### Considerations on the Path to Modernization:

Amidst the modernization maze, several crucial considerations demand attention. Understanding the diversity of product offerings, aligning modernization goals with regulatory posture, and ensuring adequate support staff are paramount. Speed of change and technical capabilities also weigh heavily in the decision-making process.

You have to consider if you possess the necessary support staff to uphold your modernization objectives? Their presence is crucial, as, without them, you may have a modernized system without anyone to maintain it. Additionally, consider the pace at which you aim to drive change within your business.

Determine whether to opt for a SaaS or a self-hosted solution. Cloud-native platforms, built on microservices and event-driven architecture, offer scalability and agility. The decision to build or buy a core banking platform hinges on control, flexibility, and feature requirements.

### **Migrating core:**

![Image](/images/blog/img12-1024x303-1.jpg)

At times, banks aim to minimize their physical space requirements and opt for a lift-and-shift approach, transitioning to macro-services. This enables businesses to fulfill their operational demands through auto-scaling while transitioning from capital expenditures to operational expenditures. However, a drawback lies in the necessity of overlays to develop contemporary front-end experiences. Despite its financial viability, the lift-and-shift method still demands significant effort in refactoring workloads for cloud optimization and crafting modern interfaces to achieve application modernization.
